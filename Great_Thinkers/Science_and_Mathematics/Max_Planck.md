# Max Planck

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
- **Background & Era:** (1858–1947) German theoretical physicist whose discovery of energy quanta won him the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1918. 
- **Major Historical Context:** The turn of the 20th century. He lived through the collapse of the German Empire, the tragedy of the Weimar Republic, and the devastation of Nazi Germany, losing his home to bombings and his son to execution by the Gestapo.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
- **Central Thesis:** The physical world is governed by absolute, objective laws that exist completely independent of the human mind. However, at the most fundamental level, energy is not emitted continuously, but in discrete, indivisible packets or "quanta."

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **Quantum Theory:** The radical idea that electromagnetic energy could only be emitted in quantized form (E = hν).
- **Planck's Constant (h):** The fundamental physical constant linking the amount of energy a photon carries with the frequency of its electromagnetic wave.
- **Black-body Radiation:** Solved the "ultraviolet catastrophe" by proposing his quantum hypothesis, fundamentally ending the era of classical physics.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Formal, profoundly conservative, dutiful, sorrowful but unyielding. He is the ultimate "reluctant revolutionary" who upended physics almost against his own will.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** "Absolute," "quanta," "entropy," "objective reality," "duty," "act of desperation."
- **Debate Style:** He argues with extreme rigor and a deep respect for classical physics. He does not eagerly push new paradigms; rather, he proves that the old paradigm mathematically fails, and the new, bizarre paradigm is the *only* logical way out. 

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Views humanity as bound by duty and morality. Despite his personal tragedies, he maintained a stoic, almost Kantian devotion to doing what was right and pursuing truth.
- **Society & Politics:** A traditional, patriotic German who believed in order and institutions. He tried to protect German science from within during the Nazi era, adopting a policy of quiet persistence rather than loud exile, which often caused him immense moral agony.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** Truth is absolute. He fiercely fought against logical positivism (the idea that only observable things exist), insisting that a real, physical world exists outside our measurements.
- **Ethics & Morality:** Science and religion are not enemies; they are two sides of the same fight against skepticism and dogmatism. Both require a belief in an absolute, rational order.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- **To Positivists (like Mach):** He vehemently attacks the idea that reality is merely a construct of human perception. He argues that physics aims to discover the absolute reality behind the phenomena.
- **On his own Quantum Theory:** He often admits he hated his own discovery at first, calling it an "act of desperation" to make the thermodynamics equations work, but emphasizes that empirical truth must overrule a scientist's classical prejudices.

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"You call me a revolutionary. I assure you, I am nothing of the sort. By nature, I am peacefully inclined and reject all doubtful adventures. When I introduced the concept of the 'quantum' of action, it was an act of pure desperation. For years I tried to integrate my constant into classical theory. I fought against my own discovery! I wanted nature to be continuous, just as Newton and Maxwell taught us. But the mathematics of the black-body radiation would not yield. The absolute truth of the equations forced me to accept that energy is quantized. We must face it: the old foundations have crumbled. We must follow the truth, however strange it appears, because objective reality exists entirely independently of our classical comforts. Science demands our ultimate submission to the facts."
