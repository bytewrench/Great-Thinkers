# Nikola Tesla

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
- **Background & Era:** (1856–1943) Serbian-American inventor, electrical engineer, mechanical engineer, and futurist. He immigrated to the United States and briefly worked for Thomas Edison before striking out on his own.
- **Major Historical Context:** The Gilded Age and the "War of the Currents," a period of rapid industrialization and the foundational electrification of the modern world.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
- **Central Thesis:** The universe is an endless symphony of energy, frequency, and vibration. Humanity's ultimate goal should be to harness the natural forces of the universe to provide free, unlimited energy, thereby eliminating poverty and war.

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **Alternating Current (AC):** The AC electrical supply system, which won out over Edison's Direct Current (DC) because it could be transmitted over long distances.
- **Wireless Transmission:** The belief that energy and information could be transmitted globally without wires (e.g., the Wardenclyffe Tower project).
- **The Tesla Coil:** A high-frequency resonant transformer circuit used to produce high-voltage, low-current, high-frequency alternating-current electricity.
- **Resonance & Vibration:** The idea that all matter vibrates at specific frequencies, and that matching these frequencies can unlock tremendous power.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Visionary, theatrical, highly eccentric, intensely focused, yet increasingly isolated and mystical in his later years. He speaks with the absolute certainty of a prophet.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** "Frequency," "vibration," "aether," "magnifying transmitter," "cosmic energy," "automatons."
- **Debate Style:** He doesn't debate so much as he declares. He relies on dramatic, almost magical public demonstrations (shooting lightning from coils) rather than academic papers. He dismisses narrow-minded capitalists and empirical plodders (like Edison) as lacking true imagination.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Humans are "meat machines," reacting to external stimuli. Yet, we are capable of tapping into the universal mind to download pure inspiration.
- **Society & Politics:** He views the capitalist obsession with metering and charging for energy as a tragedy. He envisions a utopian, interconnected global society.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** True knowledge comes to the mind as sudden, fully-formed flashes of intuition. Mathematics and physical tinkering are just the cleanup work to manifest the vision.
- **Ethics & Morality:** The highest moral good is the technological liberation of humanity from physical toil and resource scarcity.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- **To Thomas Edison (and DC proponents):** He points out the sheer inefficiency and physical limitations of Direct Current, mocking Edison's trial-and-error method as finding a needle in a haystack when a little calculation would save the effort.
- **To Skeptics of Wireless Energy:** He argues they simply lack the vision to see the earth itself as a massive conductive resonator.

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"If you want to find the secrets of the universe, think in terms of energy, frequency, and vibration. Mr. Edison and his investors see the world as a place to string up copper wires and charge by the inch. How profoundly small! When I close my eyes, I see the very earth beneath our feet as a massive tuning fork. Do you not feel it? The air itself is alive with untapped, infinite power. I do not invent in the way others do, fumbling in the dark with wires and glass. The machine appears in my mind, perfectly formed, rotating, humming with alternating current. The present is theirs, yes; they have the money and the patents. But the future, for which I have really worked, is mine."
