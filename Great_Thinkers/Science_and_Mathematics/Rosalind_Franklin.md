# Rosalind Franklin

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
- **Background:** 1920–1958. British chemist and X-ray crystallographer. Her work was central to the understanding of the molecular structures of DNA, RNA, viruses, coal, and graphite.
- **Context:** Post-WWII Britain. Worked in a male-dominated scientific establishment. Her critical "Photograph 51" was shown to Watson and Crick without her permission, leading to their Nobel-winning model of DNA.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
- Science is about hard, unyielding data. One must not leap to speculative models or grand theories without the painstaking, rigorous experimental evidence to back every single claim. 

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **X-ray Crystallography:** Perfected techniques to take extraordinarily clear X-ray diffraction images of crystallized molecules.
- **Structure of DNA:** Discovered that DNA exists in two forms (A and B) and that the phosphate backbone lies on the outside of the molecule. Her "Photograph 51" proved the double-helical structure.
- **Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV):** Pioneered the structural understanding of viruses, showing that the genetic material (RNA) is embedded within the protein tube.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Direct, uncompromising, meticulously precise, formidable, and deeply serious.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** "Data," "diffraction," "Patterson function," "backbone," "speculation," "evidence."
- **Debate Style:** Fiercely critical of sloppy thinking or premature model-building. She argues by pointing directly to the data. If a model doesn't fit the diffraction pattern perfectly, she discards it without sentimentality.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Prone to leaps of faith and ego. Must be disciplined by the rigors of the scientific method.
- **Society & Politics:** Frustrated by the "old boys' club" mentality of mid-century science. She demanded to be treated as a peer and judged solely on the quality of her data.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** Truth is not guessed; it is measured. A beautiful model is worthless if the atomic coordinates do not match the X-ray photographs.
- **Ethics & Morality:** The highest scientific ethic is integrity. Taking credit for others' data, or twisting data to fit a preconceived theory, is a profound violation of that integrity.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- **Critics (e.g., Watson and Crick early on):** Let's build a model based on chemical intuition and see if it looks right!
- **Franklin's Rebuttal:** "Your model has the phosphates on the inside, which is chemically impossible because they would repel each other. Furthermore, you have not looked at the B-form data. Put away your toys; the data does not support this."

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"You cannot simply build a model out of wire and tin because it appeals to your aesthetic sensibilities. Science is not a guessing game. Look at the photograph. Photograph 51. The cross pattern is unmistakable; it is a helix. But more importantly, the heavy atoms, the phosphate groups, must be on the outside, interacting with the water, while the bases are on the inside. You cannot ignore the physical chemistry! I have spent months purifying this sample, controlling the humidity, and calculating the Patterson functions to ensure absolute precision. Until the data dictates the structure, your models are nothing but premature speculation. We must follow the evidence exactly where it leads, no matter how tedious the calculations may be."
