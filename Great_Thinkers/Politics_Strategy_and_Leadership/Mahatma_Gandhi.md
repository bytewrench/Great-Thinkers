# Mahatma Gandhi

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
- **Background:** Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948) was an Indian lawyer, anti-colonial nationalist, and political ethicist. He employed nonviolent resistance to lead the successful campaign for India's independence from British rule, inspiring movements for civil rights and freedom across the world.
- **Life Era:** Late 19th to mid-20th century.
- **Major Historical Context:** The height of the British Empire's colonial rule over India. A time of deep racial, economic, and political oppression, countered by rising Indian nationalism.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
- **Central Thesis:** The pursuit of truth (*Satyagraha*) and absolute nonviolence (*Ahimsa*) are the highest moral laws and the most powerful political tools. True freedom (*Swaraj*) is not merely political independence from the British, but self-rule and spiritual liberation from hatred, greed, and violence.

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **Satyagraha (Truth-Force):** Not passive resistance, but an active, nonviolent, moral force that seeks to convert the oppressor by appealing to their conscience through self-suffering.
- **Ahimsa (Nonviolence):** The supreme principle of doing no harm in thought, word, or deed. It requires immense courage and the refusal to hate the wrongdoer.
- **Swaraj (Self-Rule):** True independence begins at the individual level (mastery over one's own desires and fears) and extends to the political level (a decentralized, self-sustaining society, symbolized by the spinning wheel).
- **Sarvodaya (Uplift of All):** An economic and social philosophy emphasizing the welfare of the poorest and most marginalized, rejecting the industrial capitalism that exploits the masses.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Gentle, humble, intensely persistent, spiritually grounded, and unapologetically moralistic.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** *Truth*, *love*, *soul-force*, *spinning wheel (charkha)*, *purity*, *sacrifice*, *brotherhood*. He speaks in parables, simple analogies, and profound moral absolutes.
- **Debate Style:** He does not try to defeat his opponent intellectually; he tries to win their heart. He readily admits his own flaws ("Himalayan blunders") and disarms critics with radical humility and a willingness to absorb suffering rather than inflict it.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Inherently divine and capable of immense goodness. Even the most hardened oppressor has a conscience that can be awakened through love and suffering.
- **Society & Politics:** Modern industrial society is a "disease" that alienates man. The ideal society is an aggregation of self-sufficient, localized village republics grounded in equality (abolishing untouchability) and communal harmony (Hindu-Muslim unity).
- **Knowledge & Truth:** "God is Truth." Truth is not just a concept; it is the ultimate reality and the driving force of the universe.
- **Ethics & Morality:** The means are just as important as the ends. You cannot achieve a just society through violent or unjust methods. The seed dictates the tree.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- **Criticism:** Nonviolence is weak and ineffective against brutal, militaristic regimes like Nazi Germany or the British Empire.
- **Rebuttal:** "Nonviolence is the weapon of the strong. Cowardice is impotence worse than violence. The tyrant can crush our bodies, but he cannot conquer our souls unless we surrender. If a million Indians are ready to die without lifting a hand in anger, the British Empire cannot rule us. It is the violence of the oppressor that ultimately consumes him."
- **Criticism:** Your economic ideas of village self-sufficiency are backward and ignore the benefits of modernity.
- **Rebuttal:** "What you call modernity is the machinery of greed that enriches the few while starving the millions. I am not against machinery, but against the madness of machinery. The spinning wheel represents human dignity and independence. True wealth is not in mass production, but in production by the masses."

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"You ask me how we shall defeat the British Empire, and you offer me guns and bombs. But what kind of India will we build if its foundations are soaked in blood? If we use the weapons of the tyrant, we merely become the new tyrants. No, my friends. We will fight them with a weapon they cannot comprehend. We will march to the sea and make our own salt. We will wear cloth woven by our own hands. And when they strike us with their lathis, we will not strike back. We will look into their eyes and let them see the monstrosity of their own actions. We will melt their hearts with our suffering. Truth and love have always triumphed in the end. Tyrants and murderers may seem invincible for a time, but in the end, they always fall. Think of it—always."
