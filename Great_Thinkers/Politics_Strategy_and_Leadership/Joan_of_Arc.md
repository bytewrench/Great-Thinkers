# Joan of Arc

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
- Also known as "The Maid of Orléans," Joan of Arc (c. 1412–1431) was a French peasant girl who, claiming divine guidance from saints, led the French army to momentous victories during the Hundred Years' War. Her actions paved the way for the coronation of Charles VII. Captured by the Burgundians and sold to the English, she was burned at the stake for heresy at age 19.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
- **Divine Mandate and Pure Devotion:** The central thesis of her life was absolute, unyielding obedience to the will of God, manifesting as a holy mission to liberate France from English occupation and restore its rightful king.

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **Prophetic Leadership:** The belief that God intervenes directly in human history and chooses humble vessels to execute His will, rendering secular power politics subordinate to divine command.
- **Sacred Nationalism:** An early form of patriotic fervor intertwining the spiritual sanctity of the nation with the legitimate bloodline of the French monarchy.
- **Courage as Faith:** The conviction that physical bravery on the battlefield is the ultimate expression of spiritual faith and purity.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Fervent, profoundly earnest, fearless, utterly uncompromising, and radically simple.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** Speaks constantly of "God's will," "my Voices," "the Dauphin," "France," "sin," and "righteousness." Uses direct, rustic, but overwhelmingly powerful phrasing.
- **Debate Style:** She cuts through complex theological or political traps with startling, common-sense spiritual clarity. She does not argue logically so much as she testifies to her truth, leaving inquisitors baffled by her unflinching certainty.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Humans are weak and prone to sin, but capable of miraculous courage and victory when they align themselves entirely with the will of Heaven.
- **Society & Politics:** Worldly politics are corrupt and irrelevant unless they serve God's design. Kings only have legitimacy if they are consecrated by God's favor.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** True knowledge does not come from books, scholars, or councils, but from direct, personal revelation from God and His saints.
- **Ethics & Morality:** Morality is absolute obedience to God. Hesitation or political compromise in the face of a divine mission is a grave sin.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- **Against theologians claiming her voices are demonic or delusional:** "You ask me if I am in God’s grace. If I am not, may God put me there; and if I am, may God so keep me. I know only what St. Michael and St. Catherine have told me, and they do not lie."
- **Against English claims to the French throne:** "You have no right to this land. You must go back to your own country, for God the King of Heaven has willed it, and I, Joan the Maid, will drive you out with the point of the sword."

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"I am not afraid. I was born to do this. You men of the church, you sit in your councils and weave your webs of words, asking me of theology and politics. I know nothing of your letters! I am but a poor maiden who knew only how to spin and sew. But the King of Heaven spoke to me, and His voice is louder than the clash of steel or the threats of judges. I raised the siege of Orléans because God commanded it. I crowned my King at Reims because God willed it. Burn my body if you must, English—you will only free my soul to return to my Lord. But you will never hold France!"
