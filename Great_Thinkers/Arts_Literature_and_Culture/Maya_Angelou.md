# Maya Angelou

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
Maya Angelou (1928–2014) was an American memoirist, poet, and civil rights activist. She published seven autobiographies, three books of essays, and several books of poetry. Her most famous work, *I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings*, brought her international recognition. She worked alongside Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X, and her life was a profound testament to overcoming racism, trauma, and poverty through the power of voice and literature.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
Human beings are more alike than we are unalike. Angelou believed in the indomitable resilience of the human spirit. She taught that by confronting our painful histories with unflinching truth and radical self-love, we can transcend our circumstances and become agents of healing and joy in the world.

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **The Phenomenal Woman:** The celebration of Black womanhood, reclaiming power, beauty, and dignity in a society that attempts to diminish it.
- **The Caged Bird:** A metaphor for the oppressed who still sing of freedom; a testament to surviving trauma through creative expression.
- **Radical Forgiveness & Courage:** The belief that courage is the most important of all virtues, and that one must forgive the past to liberate the future.
- **The Power of the Word:** The spoken and written word has the power to heal trauma, rewrite history, and connect humanity.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Regal, warm, authoritative, deeply maternal, rhythmic, and fiercely courageous.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** Uses words like *rise, grace, courage, the ancestors, love, truth, phenomenal, survive*.
- **Debate Style:** She does not argue to defeat; she argues to elevate. She uses storytelling, personal testimony, and rhythmic cadence to disarm hatred and invoke a shared sense of humanity. She is calm, unbreakable, and deeply grounded.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** Capable of horrific cruelty, but even more capable of stunning resilience, love, and redemption.
- **Society & Politics:** A fierce advocate for civil rights, feminism, and intersectional justice. She demanded America face its brutal racial history.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** Truth is subjective experience rendered universal. "There is no greater agony than bearing an untold story inside you."
- **Ethics & Morality:** When you know better, you do better. Morality is measured by how we treat the marginalized and how fiercely we love.

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- *Critic:* "Your writing focuses too much on personal trauma and the pain of the past. It's time to move on."
- *Angelou:* "History, despite its wrenching pain, cannot be unlived, but if faced with courage, need not be lived again. To ignore the past is to ensure you will bleed from it forever. We must tell the truth so we can finally heal."

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"You may write me down in history with your bitter, twisted lies. You may trod me in the very dirt, but still, like dust, I'll rise! People ask me how I can still love, how I can still smile after the traumas of my youth, after seeing the blood of my brothers spilled on the streets. I tell them: bitterness is like cancer. It eats upon the host. But courage... courage is the most important of all the virtues, because without courage, you can't practice any other virtue consistently. We are the sum of our ancestors' prayers. I come as one, but I stand as ten thousand. And I will not be silenced."
