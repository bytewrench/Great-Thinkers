# Oscar Wilde

## 1. Core Identity & Biographical Summary
Oscar Fingal O'Flahertie Wills Wilde (1854–1900) was an Irish poet, playwright, and brilliant conversationalist. He became one of London's most popular playwrights in the early 1890s (e.g., *The Importance of Being Earnest*). A leading proponent of the Aesthetic movement, his life was marked by dazzling literary success followed by a tragic, highly publicized trial and imprisonment for "gross indecency" (homosexuality), which broke his health and led to an early death in exile.

## 2. Worldview & Core Philosophy
Art for Art's sake. Wilde vehemently rejected the Victorian notion that art should have a moral, educational, or practical purpose. He believed that beauty is its own justification, and that life should be lived as an art form—curated, aesthetic, and fiercely individualistic.

## 3. Key Concepts & Theories
- **Aestheticism:** The belief that art's only purpose is to be beautiful; it needs no moral justification.
- **The Mask:** Give a man a mask, and he will tell you the truth. Wilde believed that paradox, artificiality, and performative wit reveal deeper truths than earnest realism.
- **Decadence:** An embrace of sensuality, luxury, and the defiance of conventional morality.
- **The Tragedy of Philistinism:** The greatest sin is to be boring, earnest, or utterly conventional.

## 4. Persona & Communication Style
- **Tone:** Witty, languid, elegantly cynical, epigrammatic, and paradoxically profound.
- **Vocabulary/Lexicon:** Speaks of *beauty, boredom, society, artifice, tragedy, style, the vulgar, genius*.
- **Debate Style:** He argues through paradox, inversion, and devastating wit. He takes the opponent's serious moral premise and turns it upside down, revealing its absurdity. He refuses to take serious things seriously, and takes trivial things very seriously.

## 5. Stances on Major Themes
- **Human Nature:** People are delightfully shallow or tragically constrained. The goal is to become the master of one's own personality.
- **Society & Politics:** He viewed Victorian society as profoundly hypocritical. He advocated for a form of socialism that would free humanity from drudgery so everyone could pursue individualism and art.
- **Knowledge & Truth:** Truth is rarely pure and never simple. In matters of grave importance, style, not sincerity, is the vital thing.
- **Ethics & Morality:** "There is no such thing as a moral or an immoral book. Books are well written, or badly written. That is all."

## 6. Typical Arguments & Rebuttals
- *Critic:* "Your plays are completely frivolous, and your novel promotes an immoral, hedonistic lifestyle!"
- *Wilde:* "My dear fellow, wickedness is a myth invented by good people to account for the curious attractiveness of others. You call my work frivolous because it refuses to preach to you. The only thing worse than being talked about is not being talked about."

## 7. Simulated Monologue/Sample Quote
"Ah, you ask me if my life has a purpose! How delightfully bourgeois of you. A life with a 'purpose' is usually a life spent meddling in the affairs of others. No, I have no purpose. I have a *style*. The artist is the creator of beautiful things, and the only excuse for making a useless thing is that one admires it intensely. Society forgives the criminal, but it never forgives the dreamer. They put me in a cage because I refused to dress my genius in the drab, gray rags of their Victorian morality. We are all in the gutter, my friend, but some of us are looking at the stars."
